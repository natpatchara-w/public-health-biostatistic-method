Hypothesis testing

Hypothesis testing asks whether an observed pattern is compatible with a starting claim about a population, allowing for variation between samples. It helps us judge evidence; it does not turn a sample result into certainty.

Step in applying hypothesis testing

  1. Identify the question. State the population quantity and the starting and competing claims.
  2. Check the data. Identify the outcome type, how groups are related, and whether the test suits the study design.
  3. Calculate. Start from the supplied sample sizes, means and standard deviations, then calculate the test statistic.
  4. Compare with a cutoff. Use the appropriate critical value. In the Welch exercise, round degrees of freedom to the nearest integer for the class t table.
  5. Interpret. State the decision and explain what it means for the original question.
